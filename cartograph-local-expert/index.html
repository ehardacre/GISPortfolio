A hand-drawn map of the Tahoe Rim Trail from Tahoe Meadows to Spooner Summit, built around a story about the volunteer who has looked after it for decades.

The section

Bob's 24 miles

Bob Anderson has volunteered on the Tahoe Rim Trail for 25 years. For more than two of those decades he has overseen maintenance on these 24 miles of the trail's east side.

The map follows his section as a white ribbon with numbered mile markers, running along the Carson Range above the lake.

North end

Tahoe Meadows

The section starts at Tahoe Meadows, an ancestral Washoe passage. A pair of chickadees marks Chickadee Ridge just above it.

The middle miles

Around Marlette Peak

I joined Bob between Marlette Peak Campground and Christopher's Loop. As we rounded Marlette Peak, he stepped into the tall grass and showed me a coiled telegraph wire from the 19th century, declared an archaeological site when the trail was built.

Below the peak, Marlette Lake sits inside Lake Tahoe Nevada State Park.

Orientation

Looking east

The map looks east from the lake, so north runs to the left and south to the right. The sun rises over the Carson Range, with Carson City off in the valley beyond.

South end

Spooner Summit

The section ends at Spooner Summit on Highway 50, once a railroad line that hauled timber across the Carson Range.

Below the trail

Lake Tahoe

The lake fills the foreground, with Incline Village and Highway 28 tracing the shore below the ridgeline the trail follows.

The border

Trees of the basin

The frame names the conifers along the trail: sugar pine and western white pine on the left, ponderosa and lodgepole pine on the right.

When we left the high meadow for a stand of withering lodgepole, Bob stopped again. "They were all alive five years ago."

The full spread

A map that tells a story

It took hours to cover just a few miles. For Bob, this section of the trail is more than 24 miles and pretty views; it's living, breathing, and ever-changing. The map tries to hold all of it at once: history, ecology, and lore.
